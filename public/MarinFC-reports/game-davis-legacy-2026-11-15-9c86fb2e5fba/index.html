Game Report: Marin 5-0 Davis Legacy (Nov 15)

Game 1 of the two-game trial. 5–0 at home against Davis Legacy on Nov 15, 2026. This recap covers the team numbers by half, a quarter-by-quarter look at the starting 11 versus the rotation, player passing with weighted turnovers, and a few things that may be worth looking at for the next game.

For the coaching staff

These are observations from the Veo match report, offered for the coach's judgment. Players are referred to by jersey number and first name.

Lineup and availability

Out injured for this game: #14 Lucien (right back), #10 Mateo (defensive mid) and #16 Joshua (center back). That is three regular starters. The back line and the holding midfield role were reshuffled to cover them, and the team played with a short bench (a 14-player squad: 11 starters and 3 bench players), partly because of the injuries. Even so, it kept a clean sheet and held Davis to 2 shots.

A fast start with the starting group

With the starting 11 on, we scored four goals between 5' and 16' (#42 Giancarlo at 5', #27 Bruce at 11', #17 Graham at 13' and #42 Giancarlo at 16') from about 5 attempts, while Davis had one shot, at 3'. That is a strong opening, and it came without three regular starters.

A quiet middle stretch

From 17.5' to 35', as several changes were made around 15'–17', neither team had a shot or a goal. Veo's speed-zone chart shows the share of time at high speed roughly halving after the first 15 minutes (about 1.2% to 0.55%, read off a graph). The bench players passed well themselves (10 of 19, 53%, in the first half against 37% for the starting 11 as a group), so this looks more like lost rhythm than any one player.

Better passing, less of the ball after halftime

Possession fell from 52% to 42%, but pass accuracy rose from 37% to 45%. In the second half we won 4 of the game's 5 corners, with Davis winning none, and outshot Davis 5–1 (Veo's attempts count). #99 Miller scored the fifth goal at 59'. None of the corners produced a goal.

Short-handed and still in control

Without #14 Lucien, #10 Mateo and #16 Joshua, the team kept a clean sheet and held Davis to 2 shots. With #54 Rylen playing CDM in front of the back line, #23 Harry had less pressure on him and stayed composed (video review). Veo credits #23 Harry with 3 of 19 passes, which is a low completed count. His other games ran from 30% to 57%, and 0 of 10 in the first half is out of line with those, so tagging may be part of it. Up front, #27 Bruce playing up top changed the game: he gave the team an explosive outlet and a rhythm to play to, which took pressure off the defense. His Davis numbers back that up: 20 of our 104 high-intensity runs (Davis had 75 in total), 4 of the team's 6 sprints, 12 duels, the top speed on the field (17.3 mph) and the second goal of the game, at 11'.

Passing is still the open item

Our 41% pass accuracy (118 of 286) is the lowest of the 11 games with team data, and the longest string was 6 passes. For the first time in a game with pass-string data we had more strings of 4+ than the opponent (5 to 3), though the last four opponents averaged about 18 of them, so Davis didn't test that side much. Davis also completed only 43% of its passes.

Read honestly, the quarters say two things. The starting 11 made the game: 4 of the 5 goals came in Q1, before any rotation, and Davis had one shot in that spell. Then the first rotation period (Q2) was the quietest stretch of the game, with no shots at either end, and team intensity dropped. It isn't a simple starters-good, bench-bad story, though. The starting 11 didn't score in Q3. The fifth goal came in Q4 from #99 Miller, one of the bench players. And the bench group completed a higher share of its passes than the starters in both halves. The likelier explanation is rhythm: the strong start ended when several changes went on at once, and the game never got that tempo back. A 4–0 lead after 16 minutes also changes how any team plays.

With three regular starters out, the bench was short, which also limited how much rotation was possible at Davis.

Forward passing and completion Pass direction

Pass completion is hardest on players asked to play the ball forward. Veo's pass-direction chart for this game has our forward passes completing at about 50% (about 53 of 105), against about 60% sideways and nearly 100% backward. A through ball that puts a teammate in on goal but isn't finished counts the same as a misplaced pass.

Worth looking at for the next game

These are observations for the coach's consideration as game 2 of the trial comes up, not instructions.

  1. Keeping the starting group together, and rotating in smaller steps

    The first 17 minutes produced 4 goals and allowed 1 shot, and Q2, when several players were rotated at once, had no shots at either end. One idea to consider is starting each half with the same 11 where possible and changing one or two players at a time, which may help keep the group's connections on the field.

  2. The first pass out of the back

    In the first half we completed 37% of our passes (54 of 145). #54 Rylen at CDM took 42 passes, the most on the team, and completed 15; #5 Brooks completed 6 of 20 and #0 Julian completed 9 of 12. A short, safe first option through #0 Julian may be one way to turn more of these into longer strings.

  3. Managing the ball with a lead

    At 4–0 our second-half possession dropped to 42%, even as our passing improved. Using more sideways and backward passes to reset (only about 8 backward passes in the half, read off a chart) is one idea to slow the game down. Our 4 second-half corners produced no goal, so a simple corner routine could be worth a look for tighter games.

  4. When everyone is back

    Ideas to consider once #14 Lucien, #10 Mateo and #16 Joshua return, each backed by Veo data. The goal behind them is ball possession, passing rhythm and skill in moving the ball: a starting group built to keep the ball and play through pressure, with #23 Harry, #20 Deacon and #17 Graham as rotation players.

    • Midfield triangle with #54 Rylen anchoring at CDM and #10 Mateo at CM, with #42 Giancarlo and #9 Santi in the mix. #54 Rylen is the team's ball-winner and engine: across the five games with player data he leads the squad in high-intensity runs (68) and is second in duels (54), with 14 tackles, and at Davis he took 42 passes at CDM, the most on the team. #10 Mateo is the most reliable passer of the group: 58 of 98 (59%) with 18.9 weighted turnovers per 70 over four games. #9 Santi matches that completion rate (34 of 58, 59%) with the lowest weighted turnovers of the midfield options (11.5 per 70) and 9.1 defensive actions per 70. #42 Giancarlo adds 9.3 defensive actions per 70, 46 duels and 2 goals at Davis, at 25 of 53 (47%). Pairing #54 Rylen's coverage with #10 Mateo's passing in the middle could give the group both the ball-winning and the possession it needs.
    • #27 Bruce up top. At Davis he had 20 high-intensity runs, a 17.3 mph top speed and the second goal at 11', and across five games he has 58 high-intensity runs, second only to #54 Rylen. A lost pass also costs less in the weighted measure up front (ST 0.8 against 1.5 at RB). If #27 Bruce sits, moving #54 Rylen up top is a secondary option, but his main value in this setup is anchoring at CDM.
    • #14 Lucien back at right back, which lets #27 Bruce stay up top. Veo has #14 Lucien at 13 of 28 (46%) with 18.1 weighted turnovers per 70 over two games, with 16 high-intensity runs in 87 minutes and a 17.4 mph top speed. That's close to #27 Bruce's 45% (32 of 71) when he plays right back, so the gain comes from what #27 Bruce adds up top more than from passing at RB.
    • #16 Joshua at center back, alongside #5 Brooks. Veo has no player data for #16 Joshua in the five games with player stats, because he was out, so there are no numbers yet to compare. For reference, the center backs who covered: #5 Brooks completed 63 of 123 (51%) with 9.7 defensive actions per 70, and #23 Harry completed 48 of 124 (39%) with 26.1 weighted turnovers per 70. #23 Harry's 3 of 19 at Davis may include tagging errors.
    • Rotation: #23 Harry, #20 Deacon and #17 Graham as rotation players could keep the pace up in stretches when the starting group needs a lift (the Q2 lull at Davis is one example), while the starting group keeps the ball and the rhythm. #17 Graham scored at 13' at Davis.
