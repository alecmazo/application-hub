The best 17 minutes of the season so far

With the starting 11 on, we scored four goals between 5' and 16' (#42 at 5', #27 at 11', #17 at 13', #42 at 16', per team staff) from about 5 attempts. Davis managed one shot, at 3'. That's the strong opening group the trial asked for, and it delivered.

Then the rotation period had no shots at either end

From 17.5' to 35', while the bench players rotated in for several starters, neither team had a shot or a goal. The lull started when the rotation began around 15'–17'. Veo's speed-zone chart shows the share of time at high speed roughly halving after the first 15 minutes (about 1.2% down to 0.55%, read off a graph). The bench players passed well themselves, completing 10 of 19 (53%) in the first half against 37% for the starters, so the lull points to lost rhythm and connections rather than any one player.

The starters didn't score in Q3; the fifth goal came in the mixed Q4

With the starters back in Q3 we had 2 shots (44', 48') but no goal. In Q4, with the bench players rotating in, #99 had 2 shots and scored at 59', and #9 had a shot at 69'. Across the two starter quarters that's 4 goals from about 7 attempts. Across the two rotation quarters it's 1 goal from 3. Davis had one shot in each half.

Better passing, less of the ball after halftime

Possession fell from 52% to 42%, but pass accuracy rose from 37% to 45%, corners went 4–0 and attempts 5–1. At 4–0 up, the game was ours to manage.

The physical side of the trial showed up

We made 104 high-intensity runs to Davis's 75. #27 had 20 of them, plus 4 of the team's 6 sprints, 12 duels, a goal, and the top speed on the field (17.3 mph). Davis had 2 shots all game.

Passing is still the open item

Our 41% pass accuracy (118 of 286) is the lowest of the 11 recorded games, and the longest string was 6 passes. For the first time in a reported game we had more strings of 4+ than the opponent (5 to 3). But the last four opponents averaged about 18 of those, so Davis didn't test that side of our game much.

Read honestly, the quarters say two things. The starting 11 made the game: 4 of the 5 goals came in Q1, before any rotation, and Davis had one shot in that spell. Then the first rotation period (Q2) was the quietest stretch of the game, with no shots at either end, and team intensity dropped. It isn't a simple starters-good, bench-bad story, though. The starting 11 didn't score in Q3. The fifth goal came in Q4 from #99, one of the bench players. And the bench group completed a higher share of its passes than the starters in both halves. The likelier explanation is rhythm: the strong start ended when several changes went on at once, and the game never got that tempo back. A 4–0 lead after 16 minutes also changes how any team plays.

Worth looking at for the next game

These are observations for the coach's consideration as game 2 of the trial comes up, not instructions.

  1. Keep the starting 11 together for the start of each half

    The first 17 minutes produced 4 goals and allowed 1 shot. If the same 11 are available for game 2 of the trial, starting both halves with them would show whether the fast start holds up against a side that keeps the ball better.

  2. Worth a look: rotating fewer players at once

    Q2 had no shots at either end, and several starters were rotated off in that window. The bench players passed better than the team average in both halves (53% and 50%). Changing one or two players at a time is one way to keep the starting group's connections on the field.

  3. First pass out of the back

    In the first half we completed only 37% of our passes (54 of 145). The back line and #54 handled a lot of those passes: #5 completed 6 of 20, #23 3 of 19 (0 of 10 in the first half, possibly partly a tagging issue), and #54 15 of 42. #0 completed 9 of 12. A short, safe first option through #0 or the 6 could turn more of these into strings.

  4. Keeping the ball when we're ahead

    At 4–0 our second-half possession dropped to 42%, even though our passing was more accurate. Using more sideways and backward passes to reset (only about 8 backward passes all half) is one way to slow the game down with a lead.

  5. Set pieces

    We won 5 corners, 4 of them in the second half, and none produced a goal. A rehearsed corner routine could pay off in tighter games.
