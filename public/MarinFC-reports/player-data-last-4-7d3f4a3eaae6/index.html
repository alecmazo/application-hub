How weighted turnovers work

The raw unsuccessful-pass count and pass % are always shown alongside the weighted figure, so the weight can be set aside.

A lost pass from a defender or a central midfielder is more costly to our passing strings than a missed final ball from a winger. In these four games, opponents produced 71 strings of four or more passes to our 20, and our possession was 41.8%. Turnovers in the back or the middle usually end a sequence and give the opponent the next one near our goal. A “miss” from a winger is often an ambitious through ball or a final ball that nearly arrives for a striker — and Veo still counts those as unsuccessful.

Weighted turnovers = unsuccessful passes × a position weight, then scaled per 70 minutes. The weights are:

The costliest turnovers are coming from the back and from the two CDM roles

Among central and defensive roles with 60 or more minutes, weighted turnovers per 70 are highest at #45 (left back, 34.1; 54 of 128, 42%, 228 minutes), #23 (center back, 26.1; 45 of 105, 43%, 241 minutes), #20 (CDM, 24.6; 18 of 41, 44%, 98 minutes — a smaller sample than the full-game regulars), and #17 (CDM, 23.1; 26 of 63, 41%, 168 minutes across all four games). #14, at right back, is next among the smaller samples (18.1; 13 of 28, 46%, 87 minutes in two games). Those are the losses most likely to break a passing string and give the ball back near our goal. In these same four games, opponents produced 71 strings of 4+ to our 20, and our possession was 41.8%.

#10 at DM and #99 at CAM looked like the only reliable passers at Newark, but that 73% mark does not fully hold across all four games

At Newark, #10 (DM) and #99 (CAM) both completed 73%. Over the four games, #10 is still the best volume passer in midfield — 58 of 98 (59%) in 222 minutes — but completed 15 of 29 (52%) at San Juan and 14 of 28 (50%) at MVLA, before 19 of 26 (73%) at Newark and 10 of 15 (67%) in 30 minutes at Folsom. #99 finished 30 of 56 (54%) in 177 minutes, swinging from 3 of 9 (33%) at MVLA to 8 of 11 (73%) at Newark. They remain among the better ball-retainers in the middle; the Newark figure is the high point, not the four-game average.

#5 is the steadiest passer among the regular center backs; #0 has the lowest weighted-turnover rate in this group

#5 completed 57 of 103 (55%) in 277 minutes, the most minutes in the sample, including 19 of 26 (73%) at MVLA. #23, the other regular center back, was at 30%, 38%, and 38% in the first three of these games, then 21 of 37 (57%) at Folsom. #0, the starting goalkeeper, completed 22 of 38 (58%) across three full games and has the lowest weighted-turnover rate among regular back-line and midfield players (8.0 per 70). Treat the goalkeeper's 12 interceptions with care — that is an unusual label for the position.

Pass-completion trends in the middle and back are uneven, not a single-game blip

#45's four-game line is 50%, 47%, 32%, and 48%, with the 32% coming on an unusually high 47 attempted passes at Newark — a volume that may be a jersey misread, and should be treated with caution. #23: 30%, 38%, 38%, 57%. #27: 55%, then 0 of 5 (0%) at MVLA (a small and odd sample in a game Veo also credited with 6 shots), 48%, 43%. At CDM, #17 completed 2 of 19 (11%) at San Juan, then 45%, 54%, and 12 of 20 (60%) at Folsom — an improving line that still finishes at 26 of 63 (41%) over 168 minutes. #20's 3 of 12 (25%) at Newark is the weak game in a 98-minute sample that was otherwise 14 of 27 (52%) at Folsom. #14, in two games at right back, was 9 of 19 (47%) and 4 of 9 (44%). In attacking midfield, #42 improved from 0 of 4 at San Juan to 9 of 14 (64%) at Folsom, but on low volume (39 passes in 159 minutes).

#9, in a hybrid striker/mid role, has the highest completion in the 60+ minute sample

#9 completed 24 of 38 (63%) in 137 minutes, with 9.7 defensive actions per 70. The role is mixed, so those turnovers are weighted at 1.0 rather than at the 1.5 central-midfielder weight or the 0.8 striker weight. That is a useful data point if #9 is used centrally in a ball-retaining look, not a claim that the role is settled.

Suggestions for the trial

This addendum does not propose a finished lineup. It is supporting data for the two-game trial already described in the main report: a ball-retaining central midfield pairing, a runner at striker, and composure from a defensive midfielder or in the first pass out of the back. If it does not help, we drop it. Names are omitted on purpose; numbers can be mapped separately. Roles and minutes remain the coach's call.

  1. Where the ball is being lost

    The central and defensive roles with the highest weighted turnovers per 70, among players with 60 or more minutes, are #45 at left back (34.1; 54 of 128, 42%, 228 minutes), #23 at center back (26.1; 45 of 105, 43%, 241 minutes), #20 at CDM (24.6; 18 of 41, 44%, 98 minutes, a smaller sample), and #17 at CDM (23.1; 26 of 63, 41%, 168 minutes). #14 at right back is 18.1 in a smaller two-game sample (13 of 28, 46%, 87 minutes). The data supports testing different personnel, or a different first-pass approach, in the #45, #23, #20, and #17 roles. Losses there are the ones most likely to end a passing string and return the ball to the opponent near our goal.

  2. A ball-retaining pair in central midfield

    Among regular midfielders, #10 at DM provides the highest completed-pass volume: 58 of 98 (59%) in 222 minutes. That profile is the closest thing in this sample to a ball-retaining central midfielder, even though the Newark 73% (19 of 26) sat above the four-game average. #99, at CAM, completed 30 of 56 (54%) in 177 minutes; 8 of 11 (73%) at Newark did not repeat at MVLA (3 of 9, 33%). #42 has the lowest weighted-turnover rate among regular midfielders (13.2 per 70) and the highest defensive-action rate in that group (10.1 per 70), with completion rising from 0 of 4 at San Juan to 9 of 14 (64%) at Folsom, on a modest 39 attempts in 159 minutes. The two CDM roles, #17 and #20, are where the middle is currently losing the ball most. If #9 is used centrally in the hybrid striker/mid role, the four-game completion is the highest in the 60+ minute sample (24 of 38, 63%), at a mixed-role weight of 1.0.

  3. Composure in the build-up

    With the costliest weighted losses coming from left back, center back, and the two CDM roles, composure on the first pass out of the back — or from the 6 — is the other half of starting a string of 4+. #5 is the steadiest passer among the regular center backs (57 of 103, 55%, 277 minutes) and leads regular defenders in defensive actions per 70 (10.6). #10 is the highest-volume completed passer at defensive mid. The data supports testing composure in the #45, #23, #20, and #17 roles, and a composed defensive-mid presence alongside a ball-retaining partner.

  4. A runner at striker

    #54 already leads the four-game sample in high-intensity runs (52 in 222 minutes, 16.4 per 70), with 4 sprints, a top speed of 15.9 mph, and the goal against MVLA. Passing from that role was 32 of 88 (36%); after the striker weight of 0.8, weighted turnovers are 14.1 per 70 — lower than the back-line and #20 figures, as the weights intend. The trial can test whether a runner's movement in that role stretches the back line enough to give midfield more time on the ball, and whether the first pass from the 9 can be safer. The current work rate is not the problem these matches point to.

How we'll measure the trial

After two games, the same measures in the main report still apply: possession, successful passes versus the opponent, and strings of 4+ versus the opponent. This page adds one more: do weighted turnovers per 70 fall in the #45, #23, #20, and #17 roles, and does midfield completion hold closer to #10's four-game 59% than to the lower Newark-only contrast?

The 17 unsuccessful passes are 6.7 per 70 unweighted — already among the lower raw rates in the 60+ minute group. At the winger weight of 0.6, that becomes 4.0 weighted turnovers per 70, the lowest figure in that group. If the same 17 misses were weighted as a central midfielder (1.5), the figure would be 10.1 per 70, still below every regular defender and below #20 (24.6), #17 (23.1), and #10 (18.9).

That is the point of the weights. A miss from the wing is often an ambitious through ball or a final ball that nearly arrives for a striker, and Veo still counts it as unsuccessful. A 50% mark from #7 should not be read the same way as 44% from #20 at CDM, 41% from #17 at CDM, or 42–43% from the back line.
